\subsection{Removing finite large jumps}
Subtract the finite large-jump process from the original local martingale:
\[
 R_t=N_t-J_t,\qquad
 J_t=\sum_{0<s\leq t\wedge T,\,|\Delta N_s|>c}\Delta N_s.
\]
The left limits give the exact identity, at every time,
\[
 \Delta R_t=\Delta N_t-\Delta J_t.
\]
For $0<t\leq T$, the jump $\Delta J_t$ equals $\Delta N_t$ when
$|\Delta N_t|>c$, and is zero otherwise. Consequently,
\[
 0<t\leq T\quad\Longrightarrow\quad |\Delta R_t|\leq c.
\]
For $t>T$, $\Delta J_t=0$ and hence $\Delta R_t=\Delta N_t$.
This bound concerns the source residual. Subtracting a predictable
projection or compensator of $J$ requires an additional jump estimate,
using announced times or conditional expectations; that estimate does not
follow merely from the displayed identity. The nested overlaps of the
closed sources and their predictable projections allow us to glue a global
predictable process $P$. Exhaustion of the stopping sequence then makes
$Q=J-P$ a local martingale. The following subsection constructs this $P$
and proves the required predictable-jump estimate.

Integrability of the jump introduced by closed stopping follows after a
further localization. Suppose $\tau\leq H$ for a deterministic $H<\infty$,
and that $\tau$ lies below both one local-martingale localizing coordinate
and the absolute strict passage at level $r$. The first condition identifies
$N_\tau$ with a bounded optional sample of a true martingale, so it is
integrable. The passage condition and existence of left limits imply
\[
 |N_{\tau-}|\leq r.
\]
Since the jump of $J$ is either the corresponding jump of $N$ or zero,
\[
 |\Delta J_\tau|
 \leq |N_\tau|+|N_{\tau-}|
 \leq |N_\tau|+r.
\]
Thus $\Delta J_\tau$ is integrable. No announcement of $\tau$ is needed,
and the boundary correction itself need not be predictable.
In the preceding common refinement the bound $\tau_n\leq H_n$ supplies
the deterministic horizon. This estimate therefore gives integrable total
variation of the closed stopped slices and permits their predictable projections.
