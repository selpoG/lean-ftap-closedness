\subsection{Deriving decomposition under an envelope}
\label{sec:interface-decomposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:decomposition-input}]
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:regular-gi-decomposition} to $Y-Y_0$, obtaining
$Y-Y_0=N+D$ with zero-start local martingale $N$ and adapted locally FV $D$.
That theorem follows from bounded-source decomposition, large-jump removal
and stopped pasting, independently of integral-range closedness.
We have $|Y-Y_0|\leq2q$ and $q\in L^2(Q)\subseteq L^1(Q)$.

Fix a finite horizon and first localize so that $N$ is a true martingale.
Stop further when $|N|$ or $\Var(D)$ first exceeds $r$.
For the closed stop $\sigma_r$, the pre-stop bounds and
$\Delta D=\Delta Y-\Delta N$ imply
\[
 \Var(D^{\sigma_r})\leq2r+2q+|N_{\sigma_r}|.
\]
Optional sampling and $q\in L^1$ make the right side integrable.
Apply the compensation theorem in Section~\ref{sec:canonical-cost} to this
process, constant after the fixed horizon. Then
\[
 M^{(r)}=N^{\sigma_r}+D^{\sigma_r}-(D^{\sigma_r})^p,
 \qquad A^{(r)}=(D^{\sigma_r})^p
\]
give a special decomposition. A zero-start predictable FV local martingale
vanishes, so the components agree on overlapping stopped intervals.
Pathwise boundedness and local finite variation make the stops exhaust each
horizon. Remove localization and paste over integer horizons to obtain the
claimed decomposition and uniqueness. Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:explicit-jump-envelope} to the now existing decomposition with envelope $2q$.
On each fixed $T$, the jumps of $M$ are bounded by an $L^2$ variable $\xi_T$.
Stop when $|M|$ first exceeds $n$ and at $T$. The closed-stop maximum is
at most $n+\xi_T$, so this dominated local martingale is a true $L^2$
martingale. Taking $T=n$ and increasing the levels yields an exhaustive
sequence by local boundedness of the paths. The jump lemma is proved for
an already supplied special decomposition using conditional expectation
and Doob's inequality; its proof does not invoke existence from the present theorem.
Finally, the good-integrator property is a condition on convergence in
probability at finite times, preserved by equivalent measures. It is this condition, not the special components, that
is transferred.
\end{proof}
