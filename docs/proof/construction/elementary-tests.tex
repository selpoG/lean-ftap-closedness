\subsection{Passing to test-uniform limits}
Let $X^n$ be the elementary integral representatives of a realized strategy
over a strongly progressive right-continuous source, with designated gain $X$.
Write $e_T$ for the capped absolute envelope at horizon $T$ and put
\[
 \Phi_{K,T}(Y)=E[e_T(K\cdot Y)],\qquad
 g_{n,T}=\sup\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{\Phi_{K,T}(X^n-X):|K|\leq1\}\bigr).
\]
These are finite-sum elementary test integrals; no general predictable
integral is used in their definition.
\begin{lemma}[Cauchy estimates and fixed-test limits]
For these representatives, $g_{n,T}\to0$ for every finite $T$, and
\[
 \sum_{r\geq0}2^{-r-1}g_{n,r+1}\longrightarrow0.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix $\varepsilon,T>0$. The representatives' Émery Cauchy property supplies a
cutoff $N$ common to every $K$, with
$\Phi_{K,T}(X^m-X^n)<\varepsilon$ for $m,n\geq N$.
Only now fix $n\geq N$ and $K$. Fixed-test errors tend to zero in probability;
extract an auxiliary subsequence in $m$ whose capped errors tend to zero
almost surely. They are bounded by one, so their expectations vanish.
The triangle inequality gives
\[
 \Phi_{K,T}(X^n-X)\leq
 \Phi_{K,T}(X^m-X^n)+\Phi_{K,T}(X^m-X)
 \leq\varepsilon+\Phi_{K,T}(X^m-X).
\]
Pass to the auxiliary limit, then take the supremum over $K$ using the common
$N$. Since $0\leq g_{n,T}\leq1$, dominated convergence for the geometric
series gives the second assertion.
\end{proof}
This is precisely convergence against the elementary tests defining the
topology in this account; no extension to general predictable tests is needed.
