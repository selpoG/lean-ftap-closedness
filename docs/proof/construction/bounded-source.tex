\subsection{The bounded-source decomposition input}
\label{sec:bounded-source}
The decomposition input used below is the following bounded form of the
Bichteler--Dellacherie theorem. We state explicitly which foundational result
is used, separately from integral-range closedness.

\begin{theorem}[Bounded good-integrator decomposition]
\label{thm:bounded-source}
Under the usual conditions, let $S$ be adapted and c\`adl\`ag with
$\sup_t|S_t|\leq B$ almost surely for a deterministic $B<\infty$.
Suppose elementary integration is continuous from uniform convergence of
bounded predictable elementary coefficients to convergence in probability
at every finite horizon. Then
\[
 S-S_0=M^S+A^S,
\]
where $M^S$ is a zero-start c\`adl\`ag local martingale and $A^S$ is zero-start,
predictable, c\`adl\`ag and locally of finite variation. The centered
components are unique up to indistinguishability. The measure remains the
original measure, and no market assumption is required.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We use the bounded case of the Bichteler--Dellacherie characterization as a
standard foundational theorem: an adapted c\`adl\`ag good integrator on a
finite interval is a local martingale plus an adapted c\`adl\`ag FV process.
The continuity hypothesis is exactly the elementary-integral criterion in
\cite[Definition 1.1 and Theorem 1.2]{BSV}; restriction to a finite interval
and time rescaling give that formulation. Almost-sure and everywhere uniform
bounds on elementary coefficients give the same criterion after modifying
coefficients on a time-zero null set under the usual conditions.
Thus, on a fixed horizon, write $S-S_0=N+D$, with zero-start components.
Here $D$ is initially only adapted. Its predictable replacement needs the
following additional argument; it is not supplied by arbitrary-decomposition
uniqueness.

Choose localizers making $N$ a true martingale up to this horizon and stop
further at the first strict passages of $|N|$ and $\Var(D)$ above $r$.
Let the resulting closed stop be $\sigma_r$. Before it both quantities are
at most $r$. At the endpoint,
\[
 |\Delta D_{\sigma_r}|
 \leq2B+|\Delta N_{\sigma_r}|
 \leq2B+r+|N_{\sigma_r}|,
 \qquad
 \Var(D^{\sigma_r})\leq2r+2B+|N_{\sigma_r}|.
\]
Optional sampling of the localized true martingale makes the last bound
integrable. These stops exhaust the fixed horizon, by pathwise boundedness
and finite variation. The compensator theorem, stated with its norm estimate
in Section~\ref{sec:canonical-cost}, applies to $D^{\sigma_r}$ and gives
\[
 S^{\sigma_r}-S_0
 =\bigl(N^{\sigma_r}+D^{\sigma_r}-(D^{\sigma_r})^p\bigr)
   +(D^{\sigma_r})^p.
\]
This is a special decomposition on each coordinate. On common stops, the
difference of two predictable FV components is a zero-start predictable
FV local martingale, hence zero. This proves compatibility and centered
uniqueness. Glue the stopped martingale coordinates and the predictable FV
coordinates, then glue over positive integer horizons. Countably many
agreements and right continuity give one indistinguishability event.
\end{proof}

The Lean proof constructs the bounded decomposition directly: the sampled
Doob rows have increments
$\Delta A_j=E[\Delta S_j\mid\mathcal F_{t_j}]$ and
$\Delta M_j=\Delta S_j-\Delta A_j$; predictable sign tests control their
variation, and common stopping, Hilbert convexification and regularization
produce compatible canonical limits. The mathematical account here takes
the stated bounded characterization as a foundational theorem. It does not
take any theorem about closedness of stochastic-integral ranges as input.
In particular, the later decomposition of general good integrators uses only
this bounded input, after removing large jumps and stopping the remainder;
it does not feed back into this proof.
